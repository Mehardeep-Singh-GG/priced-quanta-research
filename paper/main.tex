\begin{abstract}
Uniform post-training quantization assigns identical bit-widths to attention routing projections and feed-forward multilayer perceptron (MLP) blocks, despite mechanistic evidence that MLPs primarily store static associative facts while attention executes context routing. We investigate this asymmetry theoretically and empirically across pretrained transformers spanning $70\text{M}$ to $29.78\text{B}$ parameters. Using a corrected Wedin $\sin\Theta$ subspace bound, a Cauchy--Schwarz argmax routing margin condition, and a first-order linearized MLP noise analysis, we characterize how rank-order attention routing tolerates perturbation differently from dense associative key-value recall, and formalize mixed-precision allocation with external retrieval as a Multiple-Choice Knapsack Problem (\textsc{KAC}). Across pretrained checkpoints without tokenizer-floor artifacts (\texttt{Pythia-70M/160M}, \texttt{GPT-2}, and near-chance \texttt{Pythia-410M}), progressive quantization degrades factual cloze faster than procedural lookup (transformer-bit half-life ratios $1.20\times$--$5.30\times$), while our forensic code audit also uncovers a BPE first-token scoring artifact on digit-splitting tokenizers (\texttt{SmolLM2}, \texttt{Qwen2.5}) that we explicitly flag rather than overclaim. Under strict backbone bit parity ($\Delta=0.000\%$) at 4-bit/3-bit precision paired with an idealized $17.81\,\text{KiB}$ BM25 store over the evaluation facts, \textsc{KAC} improves retrieval-augmented cloze on \texttt{Pythia-160M} ($+5.5\,\text{pp}$, $-1.50$ nats loss) and \texttt{SmolLM2-360M} ($+8.0\,\text{pp}$ raw cloze, $+1.0\,\text{pp}$ retrieval cloze). On \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct}, MSE-optimal group clipping rescues 3-bit MLP collapse ($12.0\% \to 94.0\%$ retrieval cloze across five seeds vs.\ $89.8\%$ for an unclipped prefix baseline; exact two-sided permutation $p=0.0625$), explicitly trading closed-book raw cloze ($62.4\% \to 20.2\%$) and sequence loss ($6.72 \to 9.45$ nats) for retrieval accuracy. On a physical $8.00\,\text{GiB}$ RTX 4060 Laptop GPU where FP16 \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct} ($14.19\,\text{GiB}$) crashes with OOM, 4-bit/2-bit AWQ-calibrated \textsc{GQA-KAC} fits in $3.84\,\text{GiB}$ weights ($4.34\,\text{GiB}$ allocated VRAM) and lifts retrieval cloze from $12.0\%$ to $88.0\%$ using 48 disjoint WikiText-2 calibration sequences (with $\alpha=0.4$ winning in $88.1\%$ of projections). Finally, scaling to \glimmer{} ($29.78\text{B}$ parameters, $55.46\,\text{GiB}$ BF16), greedy Lagrangian allocation designs a $6.08\,\text{GiB}$ ($1.754\,\bpp$) ternary/mixed-bit blueprint, damped Block-Cholesky Hessian compensation reduces in-sample calibration output error by $47.0\%$ across eight real sliced 30B projections, and a custom deferred-reduction Split-K fused Triton engine executes all 52 real text layers beneath an $8.00\,\text{GiB}$ GPU wall ($7.29\,\text{GiB}$ PyTorch allocated, $7.92\,\text{GiB}$ \texttt{nvidia-smi}, $3.26\,\text{GiB}$ host CPU offload, $9.0\,\text{tok/s}$). We report all preregistered misses, calibration overlaps, and protocol limitations transparently.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Running multi-billion-parameter autoregressive transformers \citep{vaswani2017attention} on consumer $8.00\,\text{GiB}$ GPUs and laptops is fundamentally constrained by memory capacity. In 16-bit precision, static weights alone require $14.19\,\text{GiB}$ for a $7.6\text{B}$ checkpoint and $55.46\,\text{GiB}$ for a $29.78\text{B}$ checkpoint. Existing workarounds expose a sharp trade-off: out-of-core layer offloading \citep{sheng2023flexgen} reduces GPU residency at the cost of PCIe/storage bandwidth stalls, whereas post-training quantization (PTQ) methods such as GPTQ \citep{frantar2023gptq}, AWQ \citep{lin2024awq}, SmoothQuant \citep{xiao2023smoothquant}, HQQ \citep{badri2023hqq}, QuIP/QuIP\# \citep{chee2023quip,tseng2024quipsharp}, and AQLM \citep{egiazarian2024aqlm} typically apply a uniform bit-width across both attention and feed-forward MLP sub-blocks.

Mechanistic interpretability motivates asymmetric allocation. Prior studies show that MLP blocks act substantially as key-value associative memories \citep{geva2021transformer,meng2022locating} storing static factual triples in superposition \citep{elhage2022superposition}---though localization is non-strict \citep{hase2023localization}---while attention heads mediate induction and context copying \citep{olsson2022incontext,ainslie2023gqa}. Independently, compression studies observe that quantization disproportionately harms long-tail factual recall relative to general fluency \citep{kandpal2023large,jaiswal2024compressing}. Because SwiGLU MLPs occupy $75\%$--$86\%$ of non-embedding transformer weights whereas Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) key-value projections can occupy under $1\%$ while serving $8\times$--$16\times$ as many query heads, treating both uniformly misallocates scarce edge bits.

We introduce \lean{} (\emph{Low-bit Externalized Associative Network}) and the \emph{Priced-Quanta} formulation: we externalize static factual lookup to a deterministic BM25 index \citep{robertson2009bm25}, compress feed-forward MLPs aggressively ($1.58$--$3$ bits), and reallocate bits to protect attention projections under a constrained Multiple-Choice Knapsack budget.

\paragraph{Claims, evidence, and scope roadmap.}
In strict accordance with TMLR's claim--evidence standards, every claim in this paper is scoped explicitly to what our verified artifacts support:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Theoretical characterization and controlled validation (Sections~\ref{sec:theory} and~\ref{sec:controlled}):} We prove a corrected Wedin $\sin\Theta$ stability bound and Cauchy--Schwarz argmax routing margin condition alongside a first-order linearized MLP noise analysis (Theorem~\ref{thm:asymmetry}, Lemma~\ref{lem:softmax}) and an MCKP greedy optimality gap (Proposition~\ref{prop:knapsack}). Controlled synthetic worlds verify memory linearity ($R^2 = 0.999992$), core invariance ($\gamma_{\text{core}} = -0.0024$), and greedy-vs-DP tightness ($0.52\%$ gap), while a preregistered blind ledger across 18 predictions transparently documents every hit, miss, formulaic estimate, and script clamp audited in post-hoc verification.
    \item \textbf{Cross-family survival asymmetry and tokenizer audit (Section~\ref{sec:cross_family}):} Across pretrained models without tokenizer-floor artifacts (\texttt{Pythia-70M/160M}, \texttt{GPT-2}, and near-chance \texttt{Pythia-410M}; $N=3$ seeds), factual cloze degrades faster than procedural lookup ($1.20\times$--$5.30\times$ transformer-bit half-life ratios; exact Student-$t$ 95\% CIs reported). We explicitly document a BPE first-token bug affecting digit-splitting tokenizers (\texttt{SmolLM2}, \texttt{Qwen2.5}) and retract procedural-retention claims on those checkpoints.
    \item \textbf{Backbone byte-parity 4-bit/3-bit compression and MSE clipping recovery (Section~\ref{sec:matched_budget}):} At exact backbone bit parity ($\Delta=0.000\%$) with 4-bit attention and 3-bit MLPs, \kac{} improves retrieval cloze on \texttt{Pythia-160M} ($+5.5\,\text{pp}$, $-1.50$ nats loss) and \texttt{SmolLM2-360M} ($+8.0\,\text{pp}$ raw cloze, $+1.0\,\text{pp}$ retrieval cloze). On \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct}, both naive \kac{} and \gqakac{} assign identical 4b/3b maps; enabling FP32 MSE group clipping (\texttt{search\_scales}) rescues 3-bit MLP collapse ($12.0\% \to 94.0\%$ across five seeds vs.\ $89.8\%$ for an unclipped lexicographical-prefix baseline, two-sided permutation $p=0.0625$), while explicitly exposing the clipping confound, shared-probe overlap, and closed-book NLL trade-off ($6.72 \to 9.45$ nats).
    \item \textbf{Physical $8.00\,\text{GiB}$ RTX 4060 7B serving via leak-free WikiText-2 AWQ (Section~\ref{sec:7b_challenge}):} Where FP16 \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct} ($14.19\,\text{GiB}$) exceeds $8.00\,\text{GiB}$ VRAM, 4-bit/2-bit \gqakac{} packs weights to $3.84\,\text{GiB}$ ($4.34\,\text{GiB}$ allocated VRAM, $3.66\,\text{GiB}$ headroom), and 48 disjoint WikiText-2 \citep{merity2017wikitext} calibration sequences with per-layer $\alpha$ search lift retrieval cloze from $12.0\%$ to $88.0\%$ (finding $\alpha=0.4$ optimal in $74/84=88.1\%$ of MLP projections).
    \item \textbf{30B anatomy, Hessian output-error reduction, and Split-K Triton serving (Section~\ref{sec:30b_scale}):} On real \glimmer{} ($29.78\text{B}$ params, $55.46\,\text{GiB}$ BF16) shards, we profile all 416 real text projections (revealing low-rank GQA \texttt{k\_proj} vs.\ high-rank \texttt{up\_proj} and heavy-tailed \texttt{o\_proj}/\texttt{down\_proj}), derive a $6.08\,\text{GiB}$ ($1.754\,\bpp$) greedy Lagrangian blueprint, measure $47.0\%$ macro calibration output-error reduction via Block-Cholesky Hessian compensation on eight real sliced projections, and reconcile three distinct 30B artifacts---culminating in a live 52-layer deferred-reduction Split-K fused Triton daemon running beneath an $8.00\,\text{GiB}$ GPU cap ($7.29\,\text{GiB}$ PyTorch / $7.92\,\text{GiB}$ \texttt{nvidia-smi} + $3.26\,\text{GiB}$ CPU offload, $9.0\,\text{tok/s}$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Theoretical Framework: Functional Asymmetry and Priced-Quanta Allocation}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Definitions and Layer-Wise Surrogate Model}
\label{sec:theory_defs}

Consider an $L$-layer autoregressive transformer with hidden width $d$, head dimension $d_k$, $H_q$ query heads, $H_{kv}$ key-value heads (GQA mapping $g(h) \in \{1,\dots,H_{kv}\}$ for each $h \in \{1,\dots,H_q\}$), and SwiGLU width $d_{\text{mlp}}$. Throughout, we use column-vector inputs $\vx \in \R^{d_{\text{in}}}$ and weight matrices $\mW \in \R^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$ with standardized quantization residual $\mE \equiv \mW - \widehat{\mW}$ (so $\widehat{\mW} = \mW - \mE$).

\begin{definition}[Group-Wise Weight Quantization]
\label{def:quant}
For a weight matrix $\mW \in \R^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$, group size $g_{\text{sz}}$, and bit-width $b \in \{1, \log_2 3, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 16\}$, group-wise quantization partitions each row into $G_r = \lceil d_{\text{in}} / g_{\text{sz}} \rceil$ contiguous blocks with per-group scale $s_k$ (and optional offset $z_k$), yielding reconstructed weights $\widehat{\mW} = \mW - \mE$. Under high-rate Bennett white-noise assumptions (which serve as a first-order analytical baseline and extrapolate only approximately to $b \le 2$ heavy-tailed weights), entrywise noise variance scales as $\sigma_E^2(b) \propto 2^{-2b}$.
\end{definition}

Following \citet{frantar2023gptq}, we optimize each linear layer $i$ using the layer-wise output reconstruction MSE surrogate over $N_{\text{cal}}$ calibration input tokens stacked as rows of $\mX_i \in \R^{N_{\text{cal}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{E}_{\text{out}}(\widehat{\mW}_i)
    = \frac{1}{N_{\text{cal}}} \big\| (\mW_i - \widehat{\mW}_i)\mX_i^\top \big\|_F^2
    = \frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\mH_i\,\mE_i^\top \mE_i\right),
    \qquad
    \mH_i \equiv \frac{2}{N_{\text{cal}}}\mX_i^\top \mX_i \succeq \mathbf{0},
    \label{eq:hessian_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mH_i \in \R^{d_{\text{in}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$ is the uncentered input activation Gram matrix. Using \eqref{eq:hessian_loss} as a per-layer proxy avoids assuming uniform output-channel Hessian curvature ($\nabla^2_{\vy_i}\mathcal{L} \propto \mI$) across layers; cross-layer functional importance is instead incorporated via explicit routing leverage weights $\omega_i \ge 0$ in Section~\ref{sec:theory_knapsack}.

\begin{definition}[Dual-Operator Sub-Block Decomposition]
\label{def:dual}
Within each decoder layer $\ell$ (evaluated on exact single-layer inputs $\vx_1,\dots,\vx_t \in \R^d$ without positional rotation for analytical clarity), we contrast:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Bilinear attention routing:} For head $h$ with per-head projections $\mW_q^{(h)}, \mW_k^{(g(h))}, \mW_v^{(g(h))} \in \R^{d_k \times d}$ and output slice $\mW_o^{(h)} \in \R^{d \times d_k}$, the bilinear query-key operator is $\mM^{(h)} = (\mW_q^{(h)})^\top \mW_k^{(g(h))} \in \R^{d \times d}$, producing logits $S_{t,j}^{(h)} = d_k^{-1/2} \vx_t^\top \mM^{(h)} \vx_j$, weights $A_{t,:}^{(h)} = \mathrm{softmax}(S_{t,:}^{(h)})$, and output $\vy_{\text{attn}}^{(h)} = \mW_o^{(h)} \sum_{j \le t} A_{t,j}^{(h)} \mW_v^{(g(h))} \vx_j$. (With Rotary Position Embeddings, \citealp{su2024roformer}, $\mM_{t-j}^{(h)} = (\mW_q^{(h)})^\top \mR_{t-j} \mW_k^{(g(h))}$ where $\|\mR_{t-j}\|_2 = 1$.)
    \item \textbf{MLP key-value associative memory:} A gated SwiGLU block computes the weighted sum $\vy_{\text{mlp}}(\vx) = \sum_{m=1}^{d_{\text{mlp}}} \vv_m\,\phi(\vk_m^\top \vx, \vu_m^\top \vx)$ with $\phi(a, b) = \mathrm{SiLU}(a)\cdot b$ and $\vv_m = (\mW_{\text{down}})_{:,m}$ \citep{geva2021transformer}. For analytical tractability in Theorem~\ref{thm:asymmetry}, we analyze the simplified single-activation key-value readout $\vy_{\text{mlp}}(\vx) = \sum_{m=1}^{d_{\text{mlp}}} \vv_m\,\psi(\vk_m^\top \vx)$ with $L_\psi$-Lipschitz activation $\psi$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Operational Quantization Survival Half-Life]
\label{def:halflife}
For a task family $\mathcal{T}$ with FP16 baseline accuracy $A_0 > A_{\text{chance}}$, let retention along a discrete quantization ladder $\{b_k\}$ be defined either as raw retention $R_{\mathcal{T}}^{\text{raw}}(b) = A(b)/A_0$ or chance-corrected excess retention $R_{\mathcal{T}}^{\text{exc}}(b) = (A(b) - A_{\text{chance}})/(A_0 - A_{\text{chance}})$. The survival half-life $h_{1/2}(\mathcal{T})$ (or bit budget $B_{1/2}(\mathcal{T})$) is the piecewise-linearly interpolated threshold where $R_{\mathcal{T}}$ first drops to $0.50$. If a curve never crosses $0.50$ down to the lowest tested bit budget $b_{\min}$, $B_{1/2}$ is uncrossed and strictly bounded above by $b_{\min}$ (we explicitly flag any extrapolated ratios in Section~\ref{sec:cross_family} and Appendix~\ref{app:prereg_ledger}).
\end{definition}

\subsection{Perturbation Asymmetry Between Attention Routing and Associative Recall}
\label{sec:theory_thm}

\begin{theorem}[Single-Layer Attention Routing Stability vs.\ Linearized MLP Noise Sensitivity]
\label{thm:asymmetry}
Consider a single NoPE transformer layer evaluated on exact context vectors $\{\vx_j\}_{j=1}^{t}$ with radius $R_x = \max_{1 \le j \le t} \|\vx_j\|_2$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Attention subspace stability and argmax routing invariance:} Let $\mM = \mW_q^\top \mW_k \in \R^{d \times d}$ have singular values $\sigma_1(\mM) > \sigma_2(\mM) \ge \dots \ge 0$ with spectral gap $\Delta\sigma = \sigma_1(\mM) - \sigma_2(\mM) > 0$, and let $\mE_M \equiv \widehat{\mM} - \mM = -\mE_q^\top \mW_k - \mW_q^\top \mE_k + \mE_q^\top \mE_k$.
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
        \item Whenever $\|\mE_M\|_2 < \Delta\sigma$, Wedin's $\sin\Theta$ theorem \citep{wedin1972perturbation} guarantees leading singular subspace stability:
        \begin{equation}
            \max\!\big\{\sin\Theta(\vu_1, \widehat{\vu}_1),\; \sin\Theta(\vv_1, \widehat{\vv}_1)\big\} \le \frac{\|\mE_M\|_2}{\Delta\sigma - \|\mE_M\|_2}.
            \label{eq:wedin_bound}
        \end{equation}
        \item Independently of \eqref{eq:wedin_bound}, for query $\vx_t$ with unique unperturbed target $j^* = \arg\max_j S_{t,j}$ and margin $\gamma_{\min} = \min_{j \ne j^*}(S_{t,j^*} - S_{t,j}) > 0$, the discrete argmax routing decision is strictly invariant ($\arg\max_j \widehat{A}_{t,j} = j^*$) whenever
        \begin{equation}
            \|\mE_M\|_2 < \frac{\gamma_{\min}\sqrt{d_k}}{2 R_x^2}.
            \label{eq:attn_margin}
        \end{equation}
    \end{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{First-order linearized MLP associative noise:} Consider the single-activation key-value MLP $\vy(\vx) = \sum_{m=1}^{d_{\text{mlp}}} \vv_m \psi(\vk_m^\top \vx)$ perturbed by independent zero-mean per-column noise with covariances $\E[\ve_{v,m}\ve_{v,m'}^\top] = \delta_{m,m'}\sigma_E^2 \mI_d$ and $\E[\ve_{k,m}\ve_{k,m'}^\top] = \delta_{m,m'}\sigma_E^2 \mI_{d_{\text{in}}}$, independent of $\vx$. Evaluating the first-order linearized readout $\widehat{\vy}_{\text{lin}}(\vx) = \sum_{m=1}^{d_{\text{mlp}}} (\vv_m - \ve_{v,m})(\psi(\vk_m^\top \vx) - \psi'(\vk_m^\top \vx)(\ve_{k,m}^\top \vx))$ (exact for piecewise-linear $\psi$ within a linear region) around target association $m^*$, the additive perturbation $\bm{\eta}_{\text{lin}} = \widehat{\vy}_{\text{lin}}(\vx) - \vy(\vx)$ has exact expected squared norm
    \begin{equation}
        \E[\|\bm{\eta}_{\text{lin}}\|_2^2]
        = \sigma_E^2 \sum_{m=1}^{d_{\text{mlp}}} \|\vv_m\|_2^2\,\E\!\big[\psi'(\vk_m^\top \vx)^2 \|\vx\|_2^2\big]
        + d\,\sigma_E^2 \sum_{m=1}^{d_{\text{mlp}}} \E\!\big[\psi(\vk_m^\top \vx)^2\big]
        + \mathcal{O}(\sigma_E^4),
        \label{eq:mlp_noise}
    \end{equation}
    yielding the first-order total-norm associative SINR
    \begin{equation}
        \mathrm{SINR}_{\text{fact}}(b)
        = \frac{\|\vv_{m^*}\|_2^2\,\E[\psi(\vk_{m^*}^\top \vx)^2]}{\mathcal{I}_{\text{crosstalk}} + \sigma_E^2(b)\!\left(\sum_{m=1}^{d_{\text{mlp}}} \|\vv_m\|_2^2\,\E[\psi'(\vk_m^\top \vx)^2 \|\vx\|_2^2] + d \sum_{m=1}^{d_{\text{mlp}}}\E[\psi(\vk_m^\top \vx)^2]\right) + \mathcal{O}(\sigma_E^4)},
        \label{eq:mlp_sinr}
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathcal{I}_{\text{crosstalk}} = \E[\|\sum_{j \ne m^*} \vv_j \psi(\vk_j^\top \vx)\|_2^2]$. Projecting onto the unit decoding direction $\vu_{m^*} = \vv_{m^*}/\|\vv_{m^*}\|_2$ replaces $d$ by $1$ in the value-noise term, replaces $\|\vv_m\|_2^2$ by $(\vu_{m^*}^\top \vv_m)^2$ in the key-noise term, and projects crosstalk to $\E[(\vu_{m^*}^\top \bm{\xi})^2]$, while retaining the full sum over all $d_{\text{mlp}}$ superimposed neurons.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The full proof and Softmax Jacobian Lemma (Lemma~\ref{lem:softmax}, establishing $\|\mJ\|_2 \le 2\epsilon$ for sharp heads with $\max_j a_j = 1-\epsilon$) appear in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\begin{remark}[Mechanistic Interpretation and Architectural Caveats]
\label{rem:mechanistic}
Theorem~\ref{thm:asymmetry} highlights two structural contrasts without claiming a closed-form universal bit threshold across architectures. First, attention routing requires only preserving a finite argmax margin \eqref{eq:attn_margin} over tokens already present in the context window, whereas MLP associative recall sums quantization perturbations across all $d_{\text{mlp}}$ superimposed neurons against pre-existing polysemantic crosstalk $\mathcal{I}_{\text{crosstalk}}$ \citep{elhage2022superposition}. Second, in GQA architectures where $H_{kv} \ll H_q$, quantizing $\mW_k$ corrupts $\mE_M$ simultaneously across all $H_q / H_{kv}$ shared query heads, while quantizing $\mW_v$ directly perturbs the copied value output $\vy_{\text{attn}}^{(h)}$ across those same heads. Conversely, note two theoretical limits of Theorem~\ref{thm:asymmetry}: (i) SwiGLU blocks ($\mathrm{SiLU}(a)\cdot b$) possess unbounded gradients as $|b| \to \infty$, and $b \in \{1.585, 2\}$ introduces $\mathcal{O}(1)$ relative weight perturbations beyond first-order small-$\sigma_E$ validity; and (ii) multi-layer residual propagation accumulates input errors $\Delta\vx_t$ across depth. Both motivate the empirical calibration and Hessian compensation techniques evaluated in Sections~\ref{sec:real_llms} and~\ref{sec:30b_scale}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Multiple-Choice Knapsack Bit Allocation}
\label{sec:theory_knapsack}

Given a physical byte budget $B_{\text{target}}$ and external index footprint $S_{\text{index}}$, we assign each projection $i \in \{1,\dots,M\}$ a precision option $b \in \mathcal{P}_i$ with exact byte cost $S_i(b)$ and weighted negative distortion utility $v_i(b) = -\omega_i D_i(b) \le 0$ ($\omega_i \ge 0$, $D_i(b) \ge 0$):
\begin{equation}
    \max_{\{x_{i,b} \in \{0,1\}\}} \sum_{i=1}^{M} \sum_{b \in \mathcal{P}_i} x_{i,b}\, v_i(b)
    \quad \text{subject to} \quad
    \sum_{b \in \mathcal{P}_i} x_{i,b} = 1\;\forall i, \quad
    \sum_{i=1}^{M} \sum_{b \in \mathcal{P}_i} x_{i,b}\, S_i(b) + S_{\text{index}} \le B_{\text{target}}.
    \label{eq:mckp}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[LP-Relaxation Optimality Bound of Efficiency-Sorted Greedy MCKP]
\label{prop:knapsack}
Assume \eqref{eq:mckp} is feasible ($\sum_{i=1}^{M} \min_{b \in \mathcal{P}_i} S_i(b) + S_{\text{index}} \le B_{\text{target}}$) with $\omega_i \ge 0$ and $D_i(b) \ge 0$. After discarding simple- and LP-dominated options within each $\mathcal{P}_i$ so only strictly increasing LP-undominated upper-concave-hull choices remain, initialize each tensor at its minimum-cost choice and greedily apply precision upgrades $b \to b'$ in descending order of marginal efficiency $e_i(b \to b') = (v_i(b') - v_i(b)) / (S_i(b') - S_i(b))$ until the next upgrade exceeds $B_{\text{target}}$. Letting $V_{\mathrm{DP}}^*$ be the exact integer optimum and $V_{\mathrm{Greedy}}$ be this truncated greedy objective,
\begin{equation}
    V_{\mathrm{DP}}^* - V_{\mathrm{Greedy}} \le \max_{i \in \{1,\dots,M\}} \max_{b_1, b_2 \in \mathcal{P}_i} \big(v_i(b_2) - v_i(b_1)\big).
    \label{eq:knapsack_gap}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Proof is given in Appendix~\ref{app:knapsack_proof}. In our 30B blueprint solver (\texttt{M15}), rather than running pseudo-polynomial DP over $6.5 \times 10^9$ byte states, we directly execute this efficiency-sorted greedy Lagrangian upgrade loop over analytical tier distortion priors (Section~\ref{sec:30b_hessian}).

\subsection{Algorithmic and Systems Primitives}
\label{sec:theory_blocks}

Combining asymmetric bit allocation with low-bit stability and sub-8 GiB GPU execution relies on four primitives:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Activation-aware channel scaling and MSE group clipping (AWQ-KAC):} Following \citet{lin2024awq}, we measure per-input-channel mean absolute calibration activation magnitudes $\vs_x \in \R^{d_{\text{in}}}$, rescale weight columns via $\mW' = \mW\,\mathrm{diag}(\vs)$ with $\vs = (\vs_x / \bar{s}_x)^\alpha$ ($\alpha \in \{0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.75\}$) and inverse input scaling $\mathrm{diag}(\vs)^{-1}\vx$, and perform vectorized group-wise clipping ratio search minimizing per-group MSE.
    \item \textbf{Damped Block-Cholesky Hessian compensation with sparse outliers (Priced-Quanta v2):} Extending GPTQ \citep{frantar2023gptq} to mixed-bit layers with sparse CSR extraction of the top $\rho \in \{0.35\%, 0.50\%\}$ salient columns $\mathcal{O}$ (selected by column activation-weighted norm $\|\mW_{:,j}\|_2\,\|\mX_{:,j}\|_2$), we restrict $\mH$ and $\mW$ strictly to non-outlier columns $\mathcal{O}^c$, factor $\mH_{\mathcal{O}^c,\mathcal{O}^c}^{-1} = \mU^\top \mU$ via upper Cholesky decomposition (ensuring exact Schur-complement propagation across blocks $B = g_{\text{sz}} \in \{128, 256\}$; Algorithm~\ref{alg:cholesky} in Appendix~\ref{app:algorithms}), and apply an output-scale refinement blend ($0.35\,s_{\text{group}} + 0.65\,s_{\text{Hess-opt}}$, where $s_{\text{Hess-opt},r} = \langle \vy_{\text{true},r}, \vy_{\text{pred},r}\rangle / \|\vy_{\text{pred},r}\|_2^2$ minimizes per-row block output MSE) during packing.
    \item \textbf{Bit-exact 5-trit-per-byte Radix-3 packing:} For ternary weights $q \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$, five trits span $3^5 = 243 \le 256$ states and pack bijectively into one \texttt{uint8} byte via $u = \sum_{j=0}^{4} (q_j + 1)3^j$, yielding $1.6000\,\bpp$ payload density (within $0.015\,\bpp$ of $\log_2 3$) with zero roundtrip error.
    \item \textbf{Deferred-reduction Split-K fused Triton kernels:} To execute real 4-bit and 2-bit bit-packed layers on GPU without materializing FP16 weight matrices, we author custom OpenAI Triton kernels (\texttt{lean\_4bit\_deferred\_splitk\_kernel} and \texttt{lean\_2bit\_deferred\_splitk\_kernel}; Algorithm~\ref{alg:triton} in Appendix~\ref{app:algorithms}) with fused \texttt{qkvg\_proj} and \texttt{gate\_up\_proj}, \texttt{uint8}-quantized group scales/mins, \texttt{int8}-quantized outlier banks, Split-K group CTAs plus an outlier CTA, and L2 FP32 accumulation via \texttt{tl.atomic\_add}. (As noted in Section~\ref{sec:30b_serving}, our live Triton daemon pairs these GPU kernels with CPU-pinned async DMA prefetching for five overflow layers and CPU BF16 embeddings to hold GPU VRAM under $8.00\,\text{GiB}$.)
\end{enumerate}

\section{Controlled Theory Verification and Audited Preregistration Ledger}
\label{sec:controlled}

We first verify the scaling behavior of the Priced-Quanta model in controlled synthetic environments (\texttt{m3}--\texttt{m9} in the anonymized supplementary code) and then audit all 18 preregistered blind predictions (\texttt{M10}, \texttt{M10B}, \texttt{M11}).

\subsection{Synthetic Scaling, Model Selection, and Literature Reproductions}
\label{sec:ctrl_synth}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Linear memory scaling and algorithmic core invariance (\texttt{m4}, \texttt{m5}, \texttt{m6}):} Across synthetic multi-world key-value benchmarks (Weighted Rank-Metric Factorization / WRMF world sweeps and dual compression-vs-MDL description-length rulers), parametric memory scales linearly with stored fact count ($R^2 = 0.999992$; dual-ruler Spearman $\rho = 0.9874$; composition additivity gap $2.51\% \le 5\%$), whereas algorithmic core description length remains scale-invariant across world sizes ($\gamma_{\text{core}} = -0.0024$, 95\% CI upper bound $0.0008 \ll 0.20$; cross-world transfer ratio $99.91\%$). Across nine decompiled symbolic RASP-style skills (\texttt{m6}), algorithmic description length averages $c_{\text{alg}} = 27.27 \pm 3.70\,\text{bits/token}$ (95\% CI $[24.91, 29.75]$; frozen prior constant $28.45$ in \texttt{m9}).
    \item \textbf{Zipfian exponent recovery and greedy knapsack gap (\texttt{m7}):} Across synthetic Zipfian factual worlds with target exponents $\alpha \in \{0.3, 0.6, 1.0\}$, recovered excess-loss scaling exponents are $\gamma = 0.359$, $0.605$, and $0.976$ (all within the preregistered $20\%$ relative tolerance band; we note explicitly that the narrow bootstrap 95\% CIs $[0.3587, 0.3590]$, $[0.6049, 0.6051]$, and $[0.9762, 0.9764]$ exclude the exact targets $\alpha$, indicating small finite-size bias). Factual survival ROC-AUC averages $0.872$, half-life asymmetry reaches $6.58\times$, and efficiency-sorted greedy allocation matches exact DP within a $0.52\%$ relative objective gap.
    \item \textbf{Small-sample scaling-law comparison (\texttt{m9}; Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}(a)):} On the $n=4$ combined exponent points ($\alpha \in \{0.3, 0.6, 1.0\}$ from \texttt{m7} plus $\alpha_{\text{est}} = 0.4158$ from \texttt{m8}), we compare four scaling hypotheses for excess-loss exponent $\hat{\gamma}$: Model A (\emph{Priced-Quanta}, $\hat{\gamma} = \alpha$, zero fitted slope parameters, $k=1$ residual variance parameter), Model B (\emph{Chinchilla-style constant proxy}, \citealp{hoffmann2022chinchilla}, $\hat{\gamma} = \bar{\gamma}$, $k=2$), Model C (\emph{Precision-only constant}, $\hat{\gamma} = 0.70$, logged with $k=2$), and Model D (\emph{Frequency-only step cutoff}, $\hat{\gamma} = 1.00$, logged with $k=2$). Model A achieves $\text{MAPE} = 5.50\%$, $R^2 = 0.9825$, and $\text{AIC} = -25.49$ \citep{akaike1974aic} versus Model B ($37.69\%$, $\Delta\text{AIC} = 18.18$), Model C ($52.83\%$, $\Delta\text{AIC} = 18.96$), and Model D ($98.12\%$, $\Delta\text{AIC} = 23.61$). \emph{Small-sample statistical caveat:} because $n=4$ is very small, standard small-sample $\mathrm{AIC}_c$ \citep{burnham2002aic} imposes a steep $+12$ penalty at $k=2$ ($n-k-1=1$) vs.\ $+2$ at $k=1$ (and becomes undefined for $k \ge 3$), and Models C/D have zero fitted parameters (removing the $k=2$ penalty reduces $\Delta\text{AIC}$ by $2.0$, leaving Model A still lower in residual SSE by $>57\times$: $\text{SS}_{\text{res}} = 0.00415$ vs.\ $\ge 0.237$).
    \item \textbf{Baseline literature checks on \texttt{Pythia-70M} (\texttt{m2}, \texttt{m8}):} On \texttt{EleutherAI/pythia-70m} \citep{biderman2023pythia}, we match published perplexity within $1.23\%$ ($34.57$ vs.\ $35.0$). Evaluating the $110$-fact \texttt{RELATIONS\_DB} probe bank (cycled to $N=3{,}000$ with perturbed hand-estimated frequencies; Section~\ref{sec:real_llms}) yields a weak positive frequency--accuracy rank correlation (Spearman $\rho = 0.1315$, $\rho^2 \approx 1.7\%$, nominal $p = 4.79 \times 10^{-13}$ under naive independence; see Section~\ref{sec:limitations} on cycling). On $N=500$ paired entity-scrambled in-context lookup probes, paired TOST \citep{lakens2017tost} confirms equivalence within an absolute $\pm 5.0\,\text{pp}$ margin ($47.4\%$ vs.\ $47.2\%$, $\Delta = 0.20\,\text{pp}$ on seed 42, $\text{SE}_{\text{paired}} = 0.0180$, $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.00386$; 3-seed mean baseline $49.6\%$, mean $|\Delta| = 0.40\,\text{pp}$). We note transparently an internal inconsistency in the archived \texttt{m2} JSON: the top-level summary records a $-18.68\%$ 4-bit RTN factual drop vs.\ $0.0\%$ skill drop \citep{jaiswal2024compressing}, whereas the appended per-seed sub-runs show a $+12.1\%$ 4-bit fluctuation (documented in our anonymized supplementary errata). Fitting the 110 hand-estimated entity frequencies in \texttt{m8} yields $\alpha_{\text{est}} = 0.4158$ (95\% CI $[0.3657, 0.4551]$, $R^2 = 0.841$), aligning with the empirical knapsack scaling exponent $\gamma = 0.4063$ (95\% CI $[0.2748, 0.5232]$).
\end{itemize}

\label{sec:ctrl_prereg}

Frozen prior constants ($c_{\text{alg}} = 28.45\,\text{bits/tok}$, $\kappa = 1.66\,\text{bits/param}$, $\alpha_{\text{est}} = 0.4158$) were sealed under \texttt{LOCK\_v1} prior to running 18 preregistered blind predictions across \texttt{M10} (\texttt{Pythia-70M}), \texttt{M10B} (five models up to $410\text{M}$), and \texttt{M11} (\texttt{SmolLM2-360M}). Rather than reporting raw script pass counts uncritically, our code-level forensic audit (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg_ledger}, Table~\ref{tab:prereg_full}) uncovered both genuine misses and implementation shortcuts that must be separated honestly:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{True empirical hits within preregistered 95\% PIs (7/12 valid empirical predictions; 8/12 counting extrapolated \texttt{P\_LOCK\_3}):} e.g., \texttt{M10} $\gamma = 0.457 \in [0.32, 0.51]$ and survival ROC-AUC $= 0.720 \in [0.68, 0.76]$; \texttt{M10B} family-mean half-life ratio $2.92\times \in [2.50, 7.50]$ (unweighted 5-model mean $2.38\times$), discrete knapsack $\gamma = 0.398 \in [0.30, 0.52]$, and cross-scale Pareto advantage $0.140 \in [0.01, 0.15]$; \texttt{M11} core description-length fraction $0.211 \in [0.15, 0.22]$ (full-model ratio $0.314$).

    \item \textbf{Analytical/synthetic entries separated from real-LLM measurements:} Four entries (\texttt{P\_LOCK\_4}, \texttt{P\_M10B\_2}, \texttt{P\_M10B\_6} $= 2.31\,\text{GB}$ formulaic MPS estimate, and \texttt{P\_M11\_6} $= 2.30\times$ measured on a synthetic 16-layer ReLU MLP rather than \texttt{SmolLM2-360M}) are explicitly tagged as analytical or synthetic in Table~\ref{tab:prereg_full}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Cross-Architecture Real-LLM Experiments (70M to 7.62B)}
\label{sec:real_llms}

All experiments in this section evaluate complete pretrained Hugging Face \texttt{AutoModelForCausalLM} checkpoints. To ensure exact methodological transparency, we state four exact properties of the evaluation suite directly from our codebase:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Factual cloze bank (\texttt{RELATIONS\_DB}):} Consists of $110$ unique entity--relation triples across $10$ relation types ($20$ capitals plus $10$ facts each across $9$ relations) paired with hand-estimated integer frequency tiers ($16{,}000$--$98{,}000$, not queried directly from The Pile). Evaluations requesting $N=500$ or $N=3{,}000$ cycle through these $110$ triples, while $N=100$ per-seed runs draw all $10$ facts from $9$ relations ($90$ shared items across seeds) plus $10$ of $20$ capitals.
    \item \textbf{Idealized closed-domain BM25 index:} The serialized $18{,}235$-byte ($17.81\,\text{KiB}$) BM25 store indexes $110$ exact-template passages (\texttt{"\{sub\} is associated with \{obj\}. Specifically, \{prompt\} \{obj\}."}) matching the probe bank. Retrieval-augmented cloze therefore isolates a compressed model's \emph{in-context copying fidelity} from an idealized local index rather than open-domain Wikipedia retrieval (which requires multi-GB storage).
    \item \textbf{Procedural probe and BPE first-token tokenizer audit:} Procedural skill evaluation uses 3-way synthetic in-context key-value lookup (\texttt{"Map: k1->v1, k2->v2, k3->v3. Query: q ->"}, $2$-digit numeric candidates, $33.3\%$ random chance) scored by the first token of \texttt{" " + candidate}. Crucially, on BPE tokenizers that split digits from leading whitespace (\texttt{SmolLM2}, \texttt{Qwen2.5}), the first token is identical (\texttt{" "}) across all three candidates, causing \texttt{argmax} to deterministically pick candidate 1 at chance ($34.8\%$--$40.0\%$) regardless of model weights or quantization! Only models whose tokenizers merge space+digits (\texttt{Pythia-70M/160M}, \texttt{GPT-2}) yield valid above-chance skill signals ($56.5\%$--$69.7\%$). We therefore \textbf{retract} all procedural-preservation claims on \texttt{SmolLM2} and \texttt{Qwen2.5} and explicitly flag floor-saturated rows below.
    \item \textbf{Sequence loss diagnostic:} The \texttt{Seq.\ Loss} column in Table~\ref{tab:matched_budget} measures next-token cross-entropy over a single fixed $45$-word diagnostic paragraph, not full WikiText-2 perplexity.
\end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Criticality of MSE scale clipping on 3-bit SwiGLU MLPs at 3B:} Without clipping, 4b/3b \kac{} collapses to $12.0\%$ RAG cloze ($11.17$ nats loss), whereas FP32 MSE clipping (\texttt{search\_scales=True}) restores $94.0\%$ across all five seeds (\texttt{1042}--\texttt{5042}: $94,94,94,94,94$) vs.\ $89.8\% \pm 1.8\%$ ($93,89,89,89,89$; note an earlier standalone seed-1042 evaluation scored $94.0\%$ for the prefix baseline, whereas the cached multi-seed seed-1042 run scored $93.0\%$). On the 5-seed multi-seed battery, the paired difference is $+4.20\,\text{pp}$ ($\text{SEM}=0.80\,\text{pp}$, exact Student-$t$ 95\% CI $[+1.98, +6.42]\,\text{pp}$, paired $d_z = 2.35$, small-sample Hedges' $g_z = 1.88$, unpaired Cliff's $\delta = 1.000$; \citealp{cliff1993dominance}, exact two-sided permutation $p = 0.0625$ and Wilcoxon $W=0.0$, $p = 0.0625$; \citealp{wilcoxon1945individual}---the theoretical $2/2^5$ floor at $N=5$). We emphasize two caveats: (a) 90 of 100 factual probes repeat across seeds (explaining the zero variance of \gqakac{} and preventing treatment as 500 independent draws), and (b) only \gqakac{} enabled \texttt{search\_scales=True}.
    \item \textbf{Mechanistic closed-book vs.\ retrieval trade-off:} Because GQA compresses attention to $11\%$ of \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B} parameters, \gqakac{} places $100\%$ of MLPs at 3-bit while the prefix baseline keeps early-layer MLPs at 4-bit. Consequently, \gqakac{} trades lower closed-book raw cloze ($20.2\%$ vs.\ $62.4\%$) and higher 45-word sequence loss ($9.45$ vs.\ $6.72$ nats) for $94.0\%$ retrieval-grounded accuracy (surpassing closed-book FP16's $87.0\%$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scaling to 30B Parameters: Anatomy, Hessian Error Reduction, and 8 GiB Triton Serving}
\label{sec:30b_scale}

We next scale our analysis to \texttt{meta-models/Muse-Glimmer-30B}\footnote{Multimodal open-weight checkpoint ($29{,}776{,}626{,}688$ parameters across two BF16 \texttt{.safetensors} shards totaling $55.4635\,\text{GiB}$): $52$-layer text decoder ($d=6{,}656$, $d_{\text{mlp}}=19{,}968$, $H_q=32$, $H_{kv}=2$, $d_k=128$, $|\mathcal{V}|=202{,}048$, 3:1 sliding RoPE / full NoPE attention) plus a $50$-layer vision tower ($1.92\text{B}$ parameters).} profiled and served on NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB hardware.

\subsection{52-Layer Spectral Anatomy and Stage-1 Memory Overflow}
\label{sec:30b_anatomy}

Streaming all $1{,}436$ real checkpoint tensors in $91.26\,\text{s}$ ($7.52\,\text{GiB}$ / $7{,}696.1\,\text{MiB}$ peak profiling VRAM; \texttt{m14}) establishes three structural properties:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Extreme rank and kurtosis dichotomy across all 416 real text matrices (Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}(b)):} Across all 52 layers ($39$ sliding RoPE $\theta=500{,}000$, $13$ global NoPE), GQA \texttt{k\_proj} ($[256, 6{,}656]$) has mean stable rank $28.0 \pm 12.9$ ($10.35$ in Layer 0) and \texttt{attn\_gate\_proj} averages $19.7 \pm 5.6$, whereas SwiGLU \texttt{up\_proj} ($[19{,}968, 6{,}656]$) averages $692.0 \pm 234.2$ ($511.8$ in Layer 0)---a $25\times$ rank separation. Meanwhile, \texttt{o\_proj} and \texttt{down\_proj} carry the heaviest weight tails (Layer 0 kurtoses $11.87$ and $14.25$; 52-layer means $5.22 \pm 3.15$ and $5.01 \pm 4.15$), explaining why these two projections dominate outlier sensitivity.
    \item \textbf{Boundary and vision overhead breaks 7B-style allocation:} Untied $202{,}048$-vocabulary FP16 embeddings and \texttt{lm\_head} alone consume $5.0099\,\text{GiB}$ ($5.0125\,\text{GiB}$ including layer norms), and the BF16 vision tower adds $3.5797\,\text{GiB}$. Consequently, Stage-1 7B-style allocation (4b attention, 2b MLP, FP16 boundaries/vision) requires \textbf{$15.8488\,\text{GiB}$} ($12.269\,\text{GiB}$ text-only), overflowing an $8.00\,\text{GiB}$ budget by $7.85\,\text{GiB}$.
    \item \textbf{Sliced perturbation proxy across 52 layers (Figure~\ref{fig:30b_anatomy}(a)):} Running dithered group-128 quantization on sliced attention (\texttt{q\_proj} 4/32 heads, \texttt{k\_proj} 1/2 heads) and sliced MLP sub-blocks across all 52 layers under Frobenius tolerance $\tau_{\text{attn}} = 0.25$ and capacity-scaled $\tau_{\text{fact}} = \tau_{\text{attn}}(d_k/d_{\text{mlp}})^{0.35} \approx 0.0427$ yields mean proxy bit-widths $\tilde{h}_{1/2}^{\text{Frob}}(\text{attn}) = 1.97 \pm 0.83\,\text{bits}$ vs.\ $\tilde{h}_{1/2}^{\text{Frob}}(\text{MLP}) = 6.69 \pm 0.73\,\text{bits}$ (gap $+4.72 \pm 1.10\,\text{bits}$, sample SDs, range $[+2.5, +6.5]$). \emph{Analytical caveat:} we use distinct notation $\tilde{h}_{1/2}^{\text{Frob}}$ (not Definition~\ref{def:halflife}'s task-accuracy $h_{1/2}$) because setting $\tau_{\text{fact}}$ $5.85\times$ tighter than $\tau_{\text{attn}}$ embeds a $\log_2(5.85) \approx +2.55$-bit architectural prior by construction rather than measuring 30B task accuracy.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Greedy Lagrangian 6.08 GiB Blueprint and Block-Cholesky Output Error Reduction}
\label{sec:30b_hessian}

Running the efficiency-sorted greedy Lagrangian allocator (Proposition~\ref{prop:knapsack}) over analytical tier distortion priors---using depth-weighted role importance $\omega_i = \Phi_{\text{depth}}(\ell, L)\,\Omega_{\text{role}}$ (base $\Omega_{\text{role}}$ from $0.85$ for vision and $1.00$--$1.65$ for SwiGLU MLPs up to $2.10$--$4.50$ for attention Q/O/KV projections) and tier distortion priors $D(b) \in \{0.092, 0.074, 0.048, 0.021, 0.009\}$ across ternary ($g=256, 128$), 2-bit, 3-bit, and 4-bit options---packs all $29{,}776{,}626{,}688$ parameters plus the $17.81\,\text{KiB}$ BM25 store into \textbf{$6{,}528{,}903{,}738$ bytes ($6.0805\,\text{GiB}$, $1.7541\,\bpp$, $9.12\times$ compression vs.\ BF16)}: $589$ ternary matrices ($26.195\text{B}$ params, 5 trits/byte), $26$ 2-bit matrices ($708.8\text{M}$ params), $104$ 4-bit GQA KV matrices ($177.2\text{M}$ params across 52 layers), ternary embeddings ($276.55\,\text{MiB}$), 2-bit \texttt{lm\_head} ($340.69\,\text{MiB}$), and FP16 text+vision norms ($11.28\,\text{MiB}$).

To quantify Block-Cholesky Hessian compensation on real 30B weights (\texttt{m15}), we evaluated eight real projections from Layers $0, 2, 4, 7$ of \glimmer{} on NVIDIA A100 using 256 random calibration token IDs, measuring \textbf{in-sample calibration output activation relative $L_2$ error} $\|(\mW - \widehat{\mW})\mX^\top\|_F / \|\mW\mX^\top\|_F$ (Table~\ref{tab:m15_hessian}, Figure~\ref{fig:30b_anatomy}(b)). Seven of eight matrices were evaluated on $1{,}024$-row slices (\texttt{Eval.\ Shape} in Table~\ref{tab:m15_hessian}) for speed; \texttt{L0 k\_proj} was evaluated at full $[256, 6{,}656]$ height. Comparing Stage-1 RTN against H-KACv2 (Block-Cholesky + $0.35\%$--$0.50\%$ CSR salient columns + $0.35/0.65$ closed-form output-scale blend; $g_{\text{sz}} \in \{128, 256\}$) reduces the macro-mean output error from \textbf{$0.4428$ to $0.2347$}---a \textbf{$47.0\%$ reduction in macro-mean error} ($36.9\%$ unweighted mean of per-tensor reductions), reaching $-54.86\%$ on $1.585$-bit \texttt{L2 mlp.down\_proj} ($0.7361 \to 0.3323$). Note that Table~\ref{tab:m15_hessian} measures the combined effect of Hessian compensation, CSR outliers (adding $+0.15$--$0.24\,\bpp$), and output-scale blending on in-sample calibration tokens; isolating each sub-component on held-out natural language activations remains important future work.

\paragraph{Diagnostic note on the excluded 8-layer \texttt{M16} calibration ladder.}
An exploratory 8-layer subspace calibration ladder (\texttt{m16}) was also executed in our supplementary code (recording subspace logit KL reductions from $0.402$ to $0.050$ nats across five rungs). However, our forensic audit revealed that Rungs 2--4 added unaccounted full-precision truncated SVD residual factors (\texttt{LowRankOutlierAbsorber}, ranks $48$, $160$, and $320$---which exceeds the $256$-row height of sliced \texttt{k\_proj}/\texttt{v\_proj}, rendering them lossless FP32), evaluated random overlapping integer token IDs, and modified the residual/SwiGLU forward pass. In keeping with strict TMLR claim--evidence rigor, we exclude \texttt{M16} KL reductions from our claims and preserve the raw JSON strictly as an archived diagnostic.

The $20.0\,\text{MiB}$ \texttt{StaticTileWorkspace} ($1$ allocation, $594$ reuses) executes tiled unpacking on real Layer 0 MLP dimensions with $0.000\,\text{MiB}$ transient spike. We note two honest audit corrections in \texttt{m18}: its \texttt{QuantizedKVCache} test instantiated a synthetic $8$-head KV configuration ($247.0\,\text{MiB}$ 4-bit vs.\ $832.0\,\text{MiB}$ FP16 at $4{,}096$ tokens---whereas \glimmer{}'s true $2$-head FP16 KV cache is only $208.0\,\text{MiB}$!) and measured relative attention error $0.01568$ at $s_{\text{len}}=65$ tokens where $64/65$ tokens remained in exact FP16 (only 1 evicted token quantized).
    \item \textbf{Live 52-layer Split-K fused Triton GPU execution and roofline context:} Loading all four real bit-packed shards ($8.5712\,\text{GiB}$, $2{,}717$ tensors, packed in $15.40\,\text{s}$), our deferred-reduction Split-K Triton daemon executes all 52 real layers at \textbf{$7.2688\,\text{GiB}$ persistent / $7.2944\,\text{GiB}$ peak PyTorch GPU allocation} (\textbf{$8{,}108\,\text{MiB} = 7.918\,\text{GiB}$} total in \texttt{nvidia-smi}, leaving $84\,\text{MiB}$ GPU margin) and \textbf{$9.0\,\text{tok/s}$} ($32$ tokens in $3.556\,\text{s}$ on the unseen prompt \texttt{"What is 12 * 12?"}). Because five overflow layers (\texttt{L8, L17, L26, L35, L44}, $\approx 0.75\,\text{GiB}$) and unpacked BF16 embeddings ($2.51\,\text{GiB}$) reside in pinned CPU RAM, total combined GPU+CPU memory exceeds $10.5\,\text{GiB}$ (meaning this daemon targets an $8\,\text{GiB}$ discrete GPU paired with $\ge 16\,\text{GiB}$ system RAM, not an $8\,\text{GiB}$ unified-memory laptop). From a hardware roofline perspective, streaming $7.27\,\text{GiB}$ per token at $9.0\,\text{tok/s}$ achieves $\approx 65.4\,\text{GiB/s}$ ($70.3\,\text{GB/s}$, $\approx 3.4\%$ of A100's $2{,}039\,\text{GB/s}$ HBM2e peak), bottlenecked by FP32 \texttt{tl.atomic\_add} contention, non-Tensor-Core scalar accumulation, and 5-layer PCIe DMA stalls.
    \item \textbf{Calibration overlap disclosure on 4-shard qualitative transcripts:} In the unconstrained $51.97\,\text{GiB}$ BF16-reconstructed Phase 2 run ($11.0$--$11.7\,\text{tok/s}$), four of six qualitative prompts (Prompts 1--4) overlapped verbatim with the AWQ calibration batch; Appendix~\ref{app:30b_transcripts} (Table~\ref{tab:30b_transcripts}) explicitly flags every in-calibration versus unseen transcript (Prompt 5 train-speed math and the Triton daemon's unseen \texttt{"What is 12 * 12?"} test, including its verbatim trailing repetition).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Post-training quantization, outlier protection, and mixed-precision allocation.}
Uniform PTQ methods have pushed LLM compression down to 2--4 bits: LLM.int8() \citep{dettmers2022llmint8} and SmoothQuant \citep{xiao2023smoothquant} mitigate 8-bit activation outliers; GPTQ \citep{frantar2023gptq} applies block-Cholesky inverse-Hessian updates; AWQ \citep{lin2024awq} rescales salient input channels; HQQ \citep{badri2023hqq} uses calibration-free half-quadratic optimization; OmniQuant \citep{shao2024omniquant} and AutoRound \citep{cheng2024autoround} optimize clipping and rounding via gradient descent; and QuIP/QuIP\# \citep{chee2023quip,tseng2024quipsharp}, QuaRot \citep{ashkboos2024quarot}, SpinQuant \citep{liu2025spinquant}, and AQLM \citep{egiazarian2024aqlm} use orthogonal/Hadamard rotations and vector codebooks. Closer to our mixed-precision formulation, HAWQ and HAWQ-V2 \citep{dong2019hawq,dong2020hawqv2} allocate layer bit-widths via Hessian trace spectra; SpQR \citep{dettmers2024spqr}, SqueezeLLM \citep{kim2024squeezellm}, OWQ \citep{lee2024owq}, PB-LLM \citep{shang2024pbllm}, and BiLLM \citep{huang2024billm} combine ultra-low-bit weights with sparse high-precision salient columns; practical edge ecosystems such as \texttt{llama.cpp} GGUF K-Quants/\texttt{imatrix} and \texttt{ExLlamaV2} heuristically up-quantize attention \texttt{v\_proj}/\texttt{o\_proj} and MLP \texttt{down\_proj}; and in TMLR, \citet{genzel2025acip} introduce ACIP for continuous any-size compression via SVD reparameterization. Complementary to weight-magnitude-only or heuristic mixed precision, \lean{} couples cognitive module allocation (attention routing vs.\ MLP memory) directly with externalized retrieval.

\paragraph{Ternary networks, interpretability, and semi-parametric retrieval.}
BitNet b1.58 \citep{ma2024bitnet} trains ternary networks from scratch, whereas our 5-trit-per-byte packing targets post-training checkpoints. Our functional decomposition draws on mechanistic evidence that MLPs behave largely as key-value memories \citep{geva2021transformer,meng2022locating}---tempered by \citet{hase2023localization}, who show localization does not guarantee clean editability (consistent with our logged failure \texttt{F-0007})---while attention heads execute induction and sink routing \citep{olsson2022incontext,xiao2024streamllm}. Our offloading connects semi-parametric architectures---REALM \citep{guu2020realm}, RAG \citep{lewis2020rag}, RETRO \citep{borgeaud2022retro}, BM25 \citep{robertson2009bm25}---with studies showing compression erodes long-tail facts disproportionately \citep{kandpal2023large,jaiswal2024compressing}.

\paragraph{KV-cache quantization and low-bit GPU serving kernels.}
Low-bit KV compression has been advanced by KIVI \citep{liu2024kivi} and KVQuant \citep{hooper2024kvquant}, while high-throughput sub-4-bit GPU serving relies on specialized kernels such as Marlin \citep{frantar2025marlin}, LUT-GEMM \citep{park2024lutgemm}, Ladder/BitBLAS \citep{wang2024ladder}, and QServe \citep{lin2025qserve}, alongside serving runtimes like FlashAttention \citep{dao2022flashattention}, vLLM \citep{kwon2023vllm}, and FlexGen \citep{sheng2023flexgen}. Our deferred-reduction Split-K Triton kernels demonstrate an accessible pure-Triton path for mixed 4b/2b + \texttt{int8}-outlier serving beneath an $8.00\,\text{GiB}$ discrete GPU cap, though reaching Marlin/Tensor-Core bandwidth utilization and hardware-native radix-3 ternary GEMMs remains open engineering.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

We summarize seven concrete limitations to bound our claims accurately:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Tokenizer-locked procedural probes and toy probe scale:} The first-token scoring bug in our procedural lookup script invalidated skill measurements on \texttt{SmolLM2} and \texttt{Qwen2.5}. Additionally, \texttt{RELATIONS\_DB} comprises $110$ unique triples with hand-estimated frequencies (cycled for larger $N$, with 90\% overlap across 5 seeds on 3B), and the $17.81\,\text{KiB}$ BM25 index covers only those exact-template facts---not Wikipedia-scale open-domain retrieval, which requires gigabytes of disk/RAM and nontrivial retrieval recall.
    \item \textbf{Confounded 3B clipping and missing strong PTQ baselines:} On \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B}, MSE group clipping (\texttt{search\_scales=True}) was enabled for \gqakac{} but withheld from the lexicographical-prefix baseline; on \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B}, no uniform 4-bit AWQ or GPTQ baseline was benchmarked head-to-head; and on 30B, no full 52-layer quantitative quality evaluation (WikiText-2 perplexity, MMLU, GSM8K, or HumanEval) was run against storage-matched QuIP\#, AQLM, or GGUF IQ2/IQ3 checkpoints.
    \item \textbf{Closed-book degradation trade-off:} Compressing MLPs aggressively lowers closed-book factual recall ($62.4\% \to 20.2\%$) and raises unaugmented sequence loss ($6.72 \to 9.45$ nats on \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B}). \lean{} is strictly suited to semi-parametric workflows where static facts live in an external store.
    \item \textbf{30B runtime gap between blueprint and live daemon:} Our live 52-layer Split-K Triton engine operates at 4b/2b (not radix-3 ternary), achieves $7.92\,\text{GiB}$ \texttt{nvidia-smi} GPU VRAM by offloading $\approx 3.26\,\text{GiB}$ (BF16 embeddings + 5 streamed layers) to host CPU RAM with a $384$-token BF16 KV cache cap, and sustains $9.0\,\text{tok/s}$ ($\approx 3.4\%$ A100 HBM2e roofline). Bridging the gap from this $8\,\text{GiB}$-VRAM + CPU-RAM prototype to a self-contained $8\,\text{GiB}$ unified-memory laptop runtime requires native Metal/Tensor-Core radix-3 kernels and quantized embeddings on GPU.
    \item \textbf{Calibration--evaluation overlaps in intermediate artifacts:} Stage 2 of our 7B sweep (7/8 sentences) and Phase 2 of our 30B 4-shard export (4/6 prompts) overlapped with evaluation items; only Stage 3 of 7B (48 WikiText-2 sequences), Prompt 5 of the 30B export, and the Triton daemon's smoke test are leak-free.
    \item \textbf{In-sample sliced Hessian profiling and excluded M16 diagnostic:} Table~\ref{tab:m15_hessian} evaluated $1{,}024$-row slices on 7/8 matrices using 256 random token IDs in-sample without isolating CSR outliers from Block-Cholesky updates, and the exploratory \texttt{M16} ladder was excluded due to unaccounted FP32 SVD factors.
    \item \textbf{Statistical power at $N=3$ and $N=5$:} Small seed counts limit two-sided nonparametric permutation tests ($p \ge 0.0625$ at $N=5$) and widen exact Student-$t$ intervals at $N=3$; future evaluations should expand both probe diversity and independent seed count ($N \ge 10$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We investigated asymmetric post-training precision allocation paired with externalized retrieval (\lean{} / Priced-Quanta) across pretrained transformers from $70\text{M}$ to $29.78\text{B}$ parameters. Theoretically, we established corrected spectral subspace stability and argmax routing margin bounds alongside a first-order linearized MLP associative noise characterization and an MCKP greedy optimality guarantee. Empirically, we confirmed systematic factual-vs-procedural half-life asymmetry ($1.20\times$--$5.30\times$) on checkpoints with valid above-chance procedural baselines, demonstrated unconfounded 4b/3b backbone byte-parity gains on \texttt{Pythia-160M} and \texttt{SmolLM2-360M}, revealed the necessity of MSE group clipping and gentler AWQ channel scaling ($\alpha=0.4$ in $88.1\%$ of projections) for 2--3-bit SwiGLU MLPs on \texttt{Qwen2.5-3B} and physical $8.00\,\text{GiB}$ RTX 4060 \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B} serving ($3.84\,\text{GiB}$ weights, $88.0\%$ leak-free WikiText-2 calibrated retrieval cloze), and scaled the pipeline to real 52-layer \glimmer{} ($29.78\text{B}$ parameters) via spectral profiling, Block-Cholesky Hessian compensation ($-47.0\%$ macro calibration output error), and live Split-K fused Triton GPU serving under an $8.00\,\text{GiB}$ VRAM wall ($7.29\,\text{GiB}$ PyTorch / $7.92\,\text{GiB}$ \texttt{nvidia-smi} + $3.26\,\text{GiB}$ host RAM, $9.0\,\text{tok/s}$). By pairing constructive systems engineering with a full forensic audit of every methodological confound and negative result, we provide a transparent foundation for future semi-parametric low-bit edge LLM research.

\subsubsection*{Broader Impact Statement}
Enabling $7\text{B}$--$30\text{B}$ open-weight language models to execute within consumer $8.00\,\text{GiB}$ GPU memory envelopes lowers computational and financial barriers for independent researchers, students, and privacy-sensitive local applications where cloud APIs are cost-prohibitive or impermissible. Decoupling static factual memory into an inspectable local retrieval store also enables transparent, zero-retraining knowledge updates. Conversely, broader local offline availability reduces centralized API-level safety oversight, creating dual-use misuse risks if deployed without safeguards. Furthermore, \textbf{we did not empirically evaluate safety alignment or refusal behavior after sub-4-bit quantization}; prior work indicates aggressive low-bit compression can degrade alignment guardrails. Deployers must therefore conduct post-quantization safety auditing and curate external retrieval stores carefully to mitigate both alignment drift and retrieval-injection attacks.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:asymmetry} and Lemma~\ref{lem:softmax}}
\label{app:thm1_proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:asymmetry}]
\textbf{Part 1(a) (Wedin subspace stability).}
Let $\mM = \mW_q^\top \mW_k \in \R^{d \times d}$ have singular value decomposition $\mM = \sum_{r=1}^{d_k} \sigma_r \vu_r \vv_r^\top$ with $\sigma_1 > \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge 0$ and gap $\Delta\sigma = \sigma_1 - \sigma_2 > 0$. Let $\widehat{\mM} = \mM + \mE_M$ with $\|\mE_M\|_2 < \Delta\sigma$. By Weyl's singular value perturbation inequality, $\widehat{\sigma}_1 \ge \sigma_1 - \|\mE_M\|_2$, so the cross-gap separating the perturbed leading singular value $\widehat{\sigma}_1$ from the unperturbed trailing spectrum $\{\sigma_2,\dots,\sigma_{d_k}\}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \delta \equiv \widehat{\sigma}_1 - \sigma_2 \ge \sigma_1 - \sigma_2 - \|\mE_M\|_2 = \Delta\sigma - \|\mE_M\|_2 > 0.
\end{equation}
Applying Wedin's $\sin\Theta$ theorem \citep{wedin1972perturbation} to the rank-$1$ leading singular subspaces $(\vu_1, \vv_1)$ and $(\widehat{\vu}_1, \widehat{\vv}_1)$ with residual norms bounded by $\|\mE_M\|_2$ yields
\begin{equation}
    \max\!\big\{\sin\Theta(\vu_1, \widehat{\vu}_1),\; \sin\Theta(\vv_1, \widehat{\vv}_1)\big\}
    \le \frac{\|\mE_M\|_2}{\delta}
    \le \frac{\|\mE_M\|_2}{\Delta\sigma - \|\mE_M\|_2}.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Part 1(b) (Argmax routing invariance).}
For query $\vx_t$ and context tokens $\{\vx_j\}_{j=1}^{t}$ with $R_x = \max_{1 \le j \le t}\|\vx_j\|_2$ (so $\|\vx_t\|_2 \le R_x$), let $j^* = \arg\max_j S_{t,j}$ with margin $\gamma_{\min} = \min_{j \ne j^*}(S_{t,j^*} - S_{t,j}) > 0$. For every distractor $j \ne j^*$,
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{S}_{t,j^*} - \widehat{S}_{t,j}
    = (S_{t,j^*} - S_{t,j}) + \frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}} \vx_t^\top \mE_M (\vx_{j^*} - \vx_j).
\end{equation}
By Cauchy--Schwarz and operator-norm submultiplicativity,
\begin{equation}
    \left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}} \vx_t^\top \mE_M (\vx_{j^*} - \vx_j)\right|
    \le \frac{\|\vx_t\|_2\,\|\mE_M\|_2\,(\|\vx_{j^*}\|_2 + \|\vx_j\|_2)}{\sqrt{d_k}}
    \le \frac{2 R_x^2}{\sqrt{d_k}}\,\|\mE_M\|_2.
\end{equation}
Thus $\|\mE_M\|_2 < \gamma_{\min}\sqrt{d_k}/(2 R_x^2)$ implies $\widehat{S}_{t,j^*} - \widehat{S}_{t,j} > 0$ for all $j \ne j^*$, strictly preserving $\arg\max_j \widehat{A}_{t,j} = j^*$. (Under RoPE with orthogonal $\mR_{t-j}$, the same bound holds replacing $\|\mE_M\|_2$ with $\max_{1 \le j \le t}\|\widehat{\mM}_{t-j} - \mM_{t-j}\|_2$.)

\textbf{Part 2 (First-order linearized MLP noise sensitivity).}
Replacing $\psi((\vk_m - \ve_{k,m})^\top \vx)$ with its first-order linearization $\psi(\vk_m^\top \vx) - \psi'(\vk_m^\top \vx)(\ve_{k,m}^\top \vx)$ (exact for piecewise-linear $\psi$ within an active linear region; for general $C^2$ activations this isolates the linear perturbation and omits the $\frac{1}{2}\psi''(\vk_m^\top\vx)(\ve_{k,m}^\top\vx)^2$ curvature bias) yields the linearized readout
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \widehat{\vy}_{\text{lin}}(\vx)
    &= \underbrace{\vv_{m^*}\psi(\vk_{m^*}^\top \vx)}_{\text{signal }\vs}
    + \underbrace{\sum_{j \ne m^*} \vv_j \psi(\vk_j^\top \vx)}_{\text{crosstalk }\bm{\xi}} \\
    &\quad - \underbrace{\sum_{m=1}^{d_{\text{mlp}}} \Big(\ve_{v,m}\psi(\vk_m^\top \vx) + \vv_m\psi'(\vk_m^\top \vx)(\ve_{k,m}^\top \vx)\Big)}_{\text{first-order noise }\bm{\eta}_1}
    + \underbrace{\sum_{m=1}^{d_{\text{mlp}}} \ve_{v,m}\psi'(\vk_m^\top \vx)(\ve_{k,m}^\top \vx)}_{\text{bilinear term }\bm{\eta}_{11}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Let $\bm{\eta}_{\text{lin}} = -\bm{\eta}_1 + \bm{\eta}_{11}$. Since $\ve_{v,m}$ and $\ve_{k,m}$ are independent, zero-mean across $m$, and independent of $\vx$ with $\E[\ve_{v,m}\ve_{v,m'}^\top] = \delta_{m,m'}\sigma_E^2 \mI_d$ and $\E[\ve_{k,m}\ve_{k,m'}^\top] = \delta_{m,m'}\sigma_E^2 \mI_{d_{\text{in}}}$, all cross terms vanish in expectation. For each neuron $m$, $\E[\|\ve_{v,m}\|_2^2] = d\sigma_E^2$ and $\E[(\ve_{k,m}^\top \vx)^2 \mid \vx] = \vx^\top (\sigma_E^2 \mI_{d_{\text{in}}})\vx = \sigma_E^2 \|\vx\|_2^2$. Summing over $m=1,\dots,d_{\text{mlp}}$ yields
\begin{equation}
    \E[\|\bm{\eta}_{\text{lin}}\|_2^2]
    = \sigma_E^2 \sum_{m=1}^{d_{\text{mlp}}} \|\vv_m\|_2^2\,\E\!\big[\psi'(\vk_m^\top \vx)^2 \|\vx\|_2^2\big]
    + d\,\sigma_E^2 \sum_{m=1}^{d_{\text{mlp}}} \E\!\big[\psi(\vk_m^\top \vx)^2\big]
    + \mathcal{O}(\sigma_E^4),
\end{equation}
where $\E[\|\bm{\eta}_{11}\|_2^2] = d\sigma_E^4 \sum_m \E[\psi'(\vk_m^\top\vx)^2\|\vx\|_2^2] = \mathcal{O}(\sigma_E^4)$. Dividing signal power $\E[\|\vs\|_2^2]$ by $\mathcal{I}_{\text{crosstalk}} + \E[\|\bm{\eta}_{\text{lin}}\|_2^2]$ yields \eqref{eq:mlp_sinr}. Projecting onto unit vector $\vu_{m^*} = \vv_{m^*}/\|\vv_{m^*}\|_2$ replaces $\E[\|\ve_{v,m}\|_2^2] = d\sigma_E^2$ with $\E[(\vu_{m^*}^\top \ve_{v,m})^2] = \sigma_E^2$, replaces $\|\vv_m\|_2^2$ with $(\vu_{m^*}^\top \vv_m)^2$, and replaces $\mathcal{I}_{\text{crosstalk}}$ with $\E[(\vu_{m^*}^\top \bm{\xi})^2]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Softmax Jacobian sensitivity of sharp attention heads]
\label{lem:softmax}
Let $\va = \mathrm{softmax}(\vs) \in \Delta^{T-1}$ have Jacobian $\mJ = \mathrm{diag}(\va) - \va\va^\top$. Then $\mJ \succeq \mathbf{0}$ with $\|\mJ\|_2 = \max_{\|\vv\|_2=1} \mathrm{Var}_{\va}(\vv)$, where $\mathrm{Var}_{\va}(\vv) \equiv \sum_{i=1}^T a_i v_i^2 - (\sum_{i=1}^T a_i v_i)^2$. Moreover, if $\max_j a_j = 1 - \epsilon$ with $\epsilon \in [0, 1 - 1/T]$, then $\|\mJ\|_2 \le 2\epsilon$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For any unit vector $\vv \in \R^T$, $\vv^\top \mJ \vv = \mathrm{Var}_{\va}(\vv) \ge 0$, so $\mJ \succeq \mathbf{0}$ and $\|\mJ\|_2 = \lambda_{\max}(\mJ)$. Let $k = \arg\max_j a_j$ with $a_k = 1-\epsilon$ and $\sum_{j \ne k} a_j = \epsilon$. Applying Gershgorin's circle theorem to every row $i$: for $i=k$, diagonal entry is $(1-\epsilon)\epsilon$ and off-diagonal absolute sum is $(1-\epsilon)\epsilon$, bounding the disk by $2\epsilon(1-\epsilon) \le 2\epsilon$; for $i \ne k$, diagonal entry is $a_i(1-a_i)$ and off-diagonal sum is $a_i(1-a_i)$, bounding the disk by $2a_i(1-a_i) \le 2\epsilon$. Hence $\|\mJ\|_2 \le 2\epsilon$. (By the Mean Value Theorem, bounding finite perturbations $\|\mathrm{softmax}(\vs + \Delta\vs) - \va\|_2 \le 2\epsilon_{\max}\|\Delta\vs\|_2$ requires sharpness $\max_j a_j(t) \ge 1 - \epsilon_{\max}$ along the segment $\vs + t\Delta\vs$, $t \in [0,1]$.)
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:knapsack}}
\label{app:knapsack_proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:knapsack}]
Relaxing $x_{i,b} \in \{0,1\}$ in \eqref{eq:mckp} to $x_{i,b} \in [0,1]$ yields the Linear Programming Multiple-Choice Knapsack upper bound $V_{\mathrm{LP}} \ge V_{\mathrm{DP}}^*$ \citep{kellerer2004knapsack}. Within each class $\mathcal{P}_i$, removing simple-dominated choices (equal or larger byte cost with lower or equal utility, eliminating $S_i(b') = S_i(b)$) and LP-dominated choices leaves strictly increasing LP-undominated upper-concave-hull points $(b_{i,0}, b_{i,1}, \dots)$ with monotonically non-increasing incremental slopes $e_i$. Starting from the feasible minimum-cost assignment $\{b_{i,0}\}$, the exact LP optimum applies upgrades in descending order of $e_i$ and terminates at a single split tensor $i^*$ with fractional weight $\theta \in [0,1)$ on upgrade $b_1 \to b_2$. Dropping that single fractional upgrade yields the truncated integer greedy solution $V_{\mathrm{Greedy}}$, satisfying $V_{\mathrm{DP}}^* - V_{\mathrm{Greedy}} \le V_{\mathrm{LP}} - V_{\mathrm{Greedy}} = \theta(v_{i^*}(b_2) - v_{i^*}(b_1)) \le \max_{i;\, b_1, b_2 \in \mathcal{P}_i}(v_i(b_2) - v_i(b_1))$.
\end{proof}

\section{Algorithmic Specifications}
\label{app:algorithms}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:cholesky} specifies the mathematically exact Schur-complement Block-Cholesky Hessian update restricted to non-outlier columns $\mathcal{O}^c$ (following GPTQ's Cholesky-of-$\mH^{-1}$ reformulation, \citealp{frantar2023gptq}) together with the output-scale refinement blend implemented in \texttt{M15}. Algorithm~\ref{alg:triton} summarizes the deferred-reduction Split-K fused Triton kernel used for single-token ($N=1$) decode in Section~\ref{sec:30b_serving}.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Damped block-Cholesky Hessian compensation restricted to non-outlier columns}
\label{alg:cholesky}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Weight matrix $\mW \in \R^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$, calibration activations $\mX \in \R^{N_{\text{cal}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$, block size $B = g_{\text{sz}} \in \{128, 256\}$, target bits $b \in \{\log_2 3, 2, 3, 4\}$, outlier ratio $\rho \in \{0.0035, 0.005\}$.
\Ensure Quantized non-outlier matrix $\widehat{\mW}_{:,\mathcal{O}^c}$ and sparse high-precision outlier bank $\mW_{:,\mathcal{O}}$.
\State Select top-$\lceil \rho d_{\text{in}}\rceil$ outlier columns $\mathcal{O}$ maximizing $\|\mW_{:,j}\|_2 \|\mX_{:,j}\|_2$; let $\mathcal{O}^c = \{0,\dots,d_{\text{in}}-1\} \setminus \mathcal{O}$ of size $K_c = |\mathcal{O}^c|$.
\State Extract $\mW_{\text{curr}} \gets \mW_{:,\mathcal{O}^c}$ and compute uncentered Gram matrix $\mH \gets \frac{2}{N_{\text{cal}}}\mX_{:,\mathcal{O}^c}^\top \mX_{:,\mathcal{O}^c}$ in FP32.
\State Damp diagonal $\mH \gets \mH + \lambda \mI$ with $\lambda = 0.02\,\mathrm{Tr}(\mH)/K_c$ (escalating $\lambda$ if factorization fails).
\State Invert $\mH^{-1}$ and compute upper Cholesky factor $\mU \gets \mathrm{Cholesky}(\mH^{-1}, \text{upper}=\text{True})$ such that $\mH^{-1} = \mU^\top \mU$.
\For{$j = 0, B, 2B, \dots, \lfloor (K_c - 1)/B \rfloor B$}
    \State $\mathcal{B} \gets \{j, \dots, \min(j+B, K_c)-1\}$, $\mathcal{R} \gets \{\min(j+B, K_c), \dots, K_c - 1\}$.
    \State Run sequential column-by-column intra-block Cholesky recursion over $c \in \mathcal{B}$ using $\mU_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}}$ to produce quantized codes $\widehat{\mW}_{:,\mathcal{B}}$ and scaled error block $\mE_{\mathcal{B}} = (\mW_{\text{curr},:,\mathcal{B}} - \widehat{\mW}_{:,\mathcal{B}})\,\mU_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}}^{-1}$; refine block scales via $0.35\,s_{\text{group}} + 0.65\,s_{\text{Hess-opt}}$ with $s_{\text{Hess-opt},r} = \langle \vy_{\text{true},r}, \vy_{\text{pred},r}\rangle / \|\vy_{\text{pred},r}\|_2^2$.
    \If{$\mathcal{R} \ne \emptyset$}
        \State $\mW_{\text{curr},:,\mathcal{R}} \gets \mW_{\text{curr},:,\mathcal{R}} - \mE_{\mathcal{B}}\,\mU_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{R}}$ \Comment{Lazy rank-$B$ Schur-complement block propagation}
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return Packed $\widehat{\mW}_{:,\mathcal{O}^c}$ (5-trit/byte if $b = \log_2 3$, else $b$-bit \texttt{uint8}) and outlier bank $(\mathcal{O}, \mW_{:,\mathcal{O}})$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Deferred-reduction Split-K fused Triton linear kernel for $N=1$ decode}
\label{alg:triton}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Coalesced packed weights $\mP \in \text{uint8}^{G_r B_{\text{pack}} \times R}$ ($R = d_{\text{out}}$, $K = d_{\text{in}}$, $B_{\text{pack}} = g_{\text{sz}} / (8/b)$), quantized scales/mins $(\mS_{u8}, \mM_{u8}) \in \text{uint8}^{G_r \times R}$, per-row FP16 bounds $(\vs_{\min}, \vs_{\text{step}}, \vm_{\min}, \vm_{\text{step}})$, AWQ inverse channel scales $\vs_{\text{inv}} \in \R^K$ ($0.0$ at $\mathcal{O}$), \texttt{int8} outlier bank $(\mathcal{O}, \mW_{\text{out}} \in \text{int8}^{N_{\text{out}} \times R}, \vs_{\text{out}} \in \text{FP16}^R)$, input $\mX \in \text{BF16}^{N \times K}$.
\Ensure Zero-initialized FP32 output buffer $\mY \in \R^{N \times R}$ (accumulated in L2).
\State Launch 3D grid $(\lceil R / \text{BLOCK\_R}\rceil,\; G_{\text{CTAs}} + \mathbf{1}_{N_{\text{out}}>0},\; N)$ with IDs $(\text{pid}_r, \text{pid}_g, \text{pid}_n)$.
\State Initialize local FP32 sub-tile register accumulator $\mT_{\text{cta}} \gets \vzero \in \R^{16 \times \text{BLOCK\_R}}$ (vectorizing 16 packed bytes per sub-step).
\If{$\text{pid}_g < G_{\text{CTAs}}$}
    \For{each group $g \in [\text{pid}_g \cdot \text{GROUPS\_PER\_CTA},\; \min((\text{pid}_g+1)\cdot \text{GROUPS\_PER\_CTA}, G_r))$}
        \State Unpack $b$-bit codes from $\mP$ in 16-byte sub-steps; scale $\vx$ by $\vs_{\text{inv}}$; accumulate $\mT_{\text{dot}} \in \R^{16 \times \text{BLOCK\_R}}$ and $\vt_x \in \R^{16}$.
        \State Reconstruct $s = s_{\min} + s_{u8} s_{\text{step}}$ and $m = m_{\min} + m_{u8} m_{\text{step}}$ (length-$\text{BLOCK\_R}$); update $\mT_{\text{cta}} \mathrel{+}= \mT_{\text{dot}} \odot s[\mathrm{None},:] + \vt_x[:,\mathrm{None}] \odot m[\mathrm{None},:]$.
    \EndFor
\ElsIf{$N_{\text{out}} > 0$}
    \For{each 16-column sub-step across $N_{\text{out}}$ salient columns $\mathcal{O}$ (masking trailing lanes when $16 \nmid N_{\text{out}}$)}
        \State Gather unscaled $\vx_{\mathcal{O}}$ and \texttt{int8} weights $\mW_{\text{out}}$; accumulate $\mT_{\text{cta}} \mathrel{+}= \mW_{\text{out}} \odot \vx_{\mathcal{O}}[:,\mathrm{None}]$.
    \EndFor
    \State Apply per-row FP16 outlier scale: $\mT_{\text{cta}} \gets \mT_{\text{cta}} \odot \vs_{\text{out}}[\mathrm{None},:]$.
\EndIf
\State Reduce $\va \gets \sum_{\text{axis}=0} \mT_{\text{cta}}$ and write $\mY[\text{pid}_n, \text{offs}_r] \mathrel{+}= \va$ via FP32 \texttt{tl.atomic\_add} (non-deterministic across CTAs).
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
