Abstract
Uniform post-training quantization assigns identical bit-widths to attention routing projec-
tions and feed-forward multilayer perceptron (MLP) blocks, despite mechanistic evidence
that MLPs primarily store static associative facts while attention executes context rout-
ing. We investigate this asymmetry theoretically and empirically across pretrained trans-
formers spanning 70M to 29.78B parameters. Using a corrected Wedin sin Θ subspace
bound, a Cauchy–Schwarz argmax routing margin condition, and a ﬁrst-order linearized
MLP noise analysis, we characterize how rank-order attention routing tolerates perturbation
diﬀerently from dense associative key-value recall, and formalize mixed-precision allocation
with external retrieval as a Multiple-Choice Knapsack Problem ( KAC). Across pretrained
checkpoints without tokenizer-ﬂoor artifacts ( Pythia-70M/160M, GPT-2, and near-chance
Pythia-410M), progressive quantization degrades factual cloze faster than procedural lookup
(transformer-bit half-life ratios 1.20×–5.30×), while our forensic code audit also uncovers
a BPE ﬁrst-token scoring artifact on digit-splitting tokenizers ( SmolLM2, Qwen2.5) that we
explicitly ﬂag rather than overclaim. Under strict backbone bit parity ( ∆ = 0 .000%) at
4-bit/3-bit precision paired with an idealized 17.81 KiB BM25 store over the evaluation
facts, KAC improves retrieval-augmented cloze on Pythia-160M (+5.5 pp, −1.50 nats loss)
and SmolLM2-360M (+8.0 pp raw cloze, +1.0 pp retrieval cloze). On Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct,
MSE-optimal group clipping rescues 3-bit MLP collapse ( 12.0% → 94.0% retrieval cloze
across ﬁve seeds vs. 89.8% for an unclipped preﬁx baseline; exact two-sided permutation
p = 0 .0625), explicitly trading closed-book raw cloze ( 62.4% → 20.2%) and sequence
loss ( 6.72 → 9.45 nats) for retrieval accuracy. On a physical 8.00 GiB RTX 4060 Lap-
top GPU where FP16 Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (14.19 GiB) crashes with OOM, 4-bit/2-bit
A WQ-calibratedGQA-KAC ﬁts in 3.84 GiB weights ( 4.34 GiB allocated VRAM) and lifts
retrieval cloze from 12.0% to 88.0% using 48 disjoint WikiText-2 calibration sequences (with
α = 0.4 winning in 88.1% of projections). Finally, scaling to Muse-Glimmer-30B (29.78B
parameters, 55.46 GiB BF16), greedy Lagrangian allocation designs a 6.08 GiB ( 1.754 bpp)
ternary/mixed-bit blueprint, damped Block-Cholesky Hessian compensation reduces in-
sample calibration output error by 47.0% across eight real sliced 30B projections, and a
custom deferred-reduction Split-K fused Triton engine executes all 52 real text layers be-
neath an 8.00 GiB GPU wall ( 7.29 GiB PyTorch allocated, 7.92 GiB nvidia-smi, 3.26 GiB
host CPU oﬄoad, 9.0 tok/s). We report all preregistered misses, calibration overlaps, and
protocol limitations transparently.
1 Introduction
Running multi-billion-parameter autoregressive transformers ( Vaswani et al. , 2017) on consumer 8.00 GiB
GPUs and laptops is fundamentally constrained by memory capacity. In 16-bit precision, static weights
1

alone require 14.19 GiB for a 7.6B checkpoint and 55.46 GiB for a 29.78B checkpoint. Existing workarounds
expose a sharp trade-oﬀ: out-of-core layer oﬄoading ( Sheng et al. , 2023) reduces GPU residency at the
cost of PCIe/storage bandwidth stalls, whereas post-training quantization (PTQ) methods such as GPTQ
(Frantar et al., 2023), A WQ (Lin et al. , 2024), SmoothQuant ( Xiao et al. , 2023), HQQ ( Badri & Shaji , 2023),
QuIP/QuIP# ( Chee et al. , 2023; Tseng et al. , 2024), and AQLM ( Egiazarian et al. , 2024) typically apply a
uniform bit-width across both attention and feed-forward MLP sub-blocks.
Mechanistic interpretability motivates asymmetric allocation. Prior studies show that MLP blocks act sub-
stantially as key-value associative memories ( Geva et al., 2021; Meng et al. , 2022) storing static factual triples
in superposition ( Elhage et al. , 2022)—though localization is non-strict ( Hase et al. , 2023)—while attention
heads mediate induction and context copying ( Olsson et al. , 2022; Ainslie et al. , 2023). Independently,
compression studies observe that quantization disproportionately harms long-tail factual recall relative to
general ﬂuency ( Kandpal et al. , 2023; Jaiswal et al. , 2024). Because SwiGLU MLPs occupy 75%–86% of
non-embedding transformer weights whereas Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) key-value projections can
occupy under 1% while serving 8×–16× as many query heads, treating both uniformly misallocates scarce
edge bits.
We introduce Project LEAN (Low-bit Externalized Associative Network ) and the Priced-Quanta formu-
lation: we externalize static factual lookup to a deterministic BM25 index ( Robertson & Zaragoza , 2009),
compress feed-forward MLPs aggressively ( 1.58–3 bits), and reallocate bits to protect attention projections
under a constrained Multiple-Choice Knapsack budget.

Remark 1 (Mechanistic Interpretation and Architectural Caveats) . Theorem 1 highlights two structural
contrasts without claiming a closed-form universal bit threshold across architectures. First, attention routing
requires only preserving a ﬁnite argmax margin ( 3) over tokens already present in the context window, whereas
MLP associative recall sums quantization perturbations across all dmlp superimposed neurons against pre-
existing polysemantic crosstalk Icrosstalk (Elhage et al. , 2022). Second, in GQA architectures where Hkv ≪
Hq, quantizing Wk corrupts EM simultaneously across all Hq/Hkv shared query heads, while quantizing Wv
directly perturbs the copied value output y(h)
attn across those same heads. Conversely, note two theoretical limits
of Theorem 1: (i) SwiGLU blocks ( SiLU(a) ·b) possess unbounded gradients as |b| → ∞ , and b ∈ { 1.585, 2}
introduces O(1) relative weight perturbations beyond ﬁrst-order small-σE validity; and (ii) multi-layer residual
propagation accumulates input errors ∆xt across depth. Both motivate the empirical calibration and Hessian
compensation techniques evaluated in Sections 4 and 5.

2.4 Algorithmic and Systems Primitives
Combining asymmetric bit allocation with low-bit stability and sub-8 GiB GPU execution relies on four
primitives:

4 Cross-Architecture Real-LLM Experiments (70M to 7.62B)
All experiments in this section evaluate complete pretrained Hugging Face AutoModelForCausalLM check-
points. To ensure exact methodological transparency, we state four exact properties of the evaluation suite
directly from our codebase:
• F actual cloze bank ( RELATIONS_DB): Consists of 110 unique entity–relation triples across 10 relation
types (20 capitals plus 10 facts each across 9 relations) paired with hand-estimated integer frequency tiers
(16,000–98,000, not queried directly from The Pile). Evaluations requesting N = 500 or N = 3,000 cycle
through these 110 triples, while N = 100 per-seed runs draw all 10 facts from 9 relations (90 shared items
across seeds) plus 10 of 20 capitals.
• Idealized closed-domain BM25 index: The serialized 18,235-byte ( 17.81 KiB) BM25 store in-
dexes 110 exact-template passages ( "{sub} is associated with {obj}. Specifically, {prompt}
{obj}.") matching the probe bank. Retrieval-augmented cloze therefore isolates a compressed model’s in-
context copying ﬁdelity from an idealized local index rather than open-domain Wikipedia retrieval (which
requires multi-GB storage).
• Procedural probe and BPE ﬁrst-token tokenizer audit: Procedural skill evaluation uses 3-way
synthetic in-context key-value lookup ( "Map: k1->v1, k2->v2, k3->v3. Query: q ->" , 2-digit nu-
meric candidates, 33.3% random chance) scored by the ﬁrst token of " " + candidate . Crucially, on
BPE tokenizers that split digits from leading whitespace ( SmolLM2, Qwen2.5), the ﬁrst token is identical
(" " ) across all three candidates, causing argmax to deterministically pick candidate 1 at chance ( 34.8%–
40.0%) regardless of model weights or quantization! Only models whose tokenizers merge space+digits
(Pythia-70M/160M, GPT-2) yield valid above-chance skill signals ( 56.5%–69.7%). We therefore retract all
procedural-preservation claims on SmolLM2 and Qwen2.5 and explicitly ﬂag ﬂoor-saturated rows below.
• Sequence loss diagnostic: The Seq. Loss column in Table 2 measures next-token cross-entropy over
a single ﬁxed 45-word diagnostic paragraph, not full WikiText-2 perplexity.

TMLR claim–evidence rigor, we exclude M16 KL reductions from our claims and preserve the raw JSON
strictly as an archived diagnostic.

6 Related Work
Post-training quantization, outlier protection, and mixed-precision allocation. Uniform PTQ
methods have pushed LLM compression down to 2–4 bits: LLM.int8() ( Dettmers et al. , 2022) and
SmoothQuant ( Xiao et al. , 2023) mitigate 8-bit activation outliers; GPTQ ( Frantar et al. , 2023) applies
block-Cholesky inverse-Hessian updates; A WQ (Lin et al. , 2024) rescales salient input channels; HQQ ( Badri
& Shaji , 2023) uses calibration-free half-quadratic optimization; OmniQuant ( Shao et al. , 2024) and Au-
toRound ( Cheng et al. , 2024) optimize clipping and rounding via gradient descent; and QuIP/QuIP# ( Chee
et al. , 2023; Tseng et al. , 2024), QuaRot ( Ashkboos et al. , 2024), SpinQuant ( Liu et al. , 2025), and AQLM
(Egiazarian et al. , 2024) use orthogonal/Hadamard rotations and vector codebooks. Closer to our mixed-
precision formulation, HA WQ and HA WQ-V2 (Dong et al. , 2019; 2020) allocate layer bit-widths via Hessian
trace spectra; SpQR ( Dettmers et al. , 2024), SqueezeLLM ( Kim et al. , 2024), OWQ ( Lee et al. , 2024),
PB-LLM ( Shang et al. , 2024), and BiLLM ( Huang et al. , 2024) combine ultra-low-bit weights with sparse
high-precision salient columns; practical edge ecosystems such as llama.cpp GGUF K-Quants/imatrix and
ExLlamaV2 heuristically up-quantize attention v_proj/o_proj and MLP down_proj; and in TMLR, Genzel
et al. (2025) introduce ACIP for continuous any-size compression via SVD reparameterization. Comple-
mentary to weight-magnitude-only or heuristic mixed precision, Project LEAN couples cognitive module
allocation (attention routing vs. MLP memory) directly with externalized retrieval.
T ernary networks, interpretability , and semi-parametric retrieval. BitNet b1.58 ( Ma et al. , 2024)
trains ternary networks from scratch, whereas our 5-trit-per-byte packing targets post-training checkpoints.
Our functional decomposition draws on mechanistic evidence that MLPs behave largely as key-value mem-
ories ( Geva et al. , 2021; Meng et al. , 2022)—tempered by Hase et al. (2023), who show localization does
not guarantee clean editability (consistent with our logged failure F-0007)—while attention heads execute

7 Limitations and Future Work
We summarize seven concrete limitations to bound our claims accurately:
1. T okenizer-locked procedural probes and toy probe scale: The ﬁrst-token scoring bug in our proce-
dural lookup script invalidated skill measurements on SmolLM2 and Qwen2.5. Additionally, RELATIONS_DB
comprises 110 unique triples with hand-estimated frequencies (cycled for larger N , with 90% overlap across
5 seeds on 3B), and the 17.81 KiB BM25 index covers only those exact-template facts—not Wikipedia-
scale open-domain retrieval, which requires gigabytes of disk/RAM and nontrivial retrieval recall.
2. Confounded 3B clipping and missing strong PTQ baselines: On Qwen2.5-3B, MSE group clipping
(search_scales=True) was enabled for GQA-KAC but withheld from the lexicographical-preﬁx baseline;
on Qwen2.5-7B, no uniform 4-bit A WQ or GPTQ baseline was benchmarked head-to-head; and on 30B,
no full 52-layer quantitative quality evaluation (WikiText-2 perplexity, MMLU, GSM8K, or HumanEval)
was run against storage-matched QuIP#, AQLM, or GGUF IQ2/IQ3 checkpoints.
3. Closed-book degradation trade-oﬀ: Compressing MLPs aggressively lowers closed-book factual recall
(62.4% → 20.2%) and raises unaugmented sequence loss ( 6.72 → 9.45 nats on Qwen2.5-3B). Project
LEAN is strictly suited to semi-parametric workﬂows where static facts live in an external store.
4. 30B runtime gap between blueprint and live daemon: Our live 52-layer Split-K Triton engine
operates at 4b/2b (not radix-3 ternary), achieves 7.92 GiB nvidia-smi GPU VRAM by oﬄoading ≈
3.26 GiB (BF16 embeddings + 5 streamed layers) to host CPU RAM with a 384-token BF16 KV cache cap,
and sustains 9.0 tok/s ( ≈ 3.4% A100 HBM2e rooﬂine). Bridging the gap from this 8 GiB-VRAM + CPU-
RAM prototype to a self-contained 8 GiB uniﬁed-memory laptop runtime requires native Metal/Tensor-
Core radix-3 kernels and quantized embeddings on GPU.
5. Calibration–evaluation overlaps in intermediate artifacts: Stage 2 of our 7B sweep (7/8 sentences)
and Phase 2 of our 30B 4-shard export (4/6 prompts) overlapped with evaluation items; only Stage 3
of 7B (48 WikiText-2 sequences), Prompt 5 of the 30B export, and the Triton daemon’s smoke test are
leak-free.
6. In-sample sliced Hessian proﬁling and excluded M16 diagnostic: Table 4 evaluated 1,024-row
slices on 7/8 matrices using 256 random token IDs in-sample without isolating CSR outliers from Block-
Cholesky updates, and the exploratory M16 ladder was excluded due to unaccounted FP32 SVD factors.
7. Statistical power at N = 3 and N = 5: Small seed counts limit two-sided nonparametric permutation
tests (p ≥ 0.0625 at N = 5 ) and widen exact Student- t intervals at N = 3 ; future evaluations should
expand both probe diversity and independent seed count ( N ≥ 10).

8 Conclusion
We investigated asymmetric post-training precision allocation paired with externalized retrieval ( Project
LEAN / Priced-Quanta) across pretrained transformers from 70M to 29.78B parameters. Theoretically, we
established corrected spectral subspace stability and argmax routing margin bounds alongside a ﬁrst-order
linearized MLP associative noise characterization and an MCKP greedy optimality guarantee. Empiri-
cally, we conﬁrmed systematic factual-vs-procedural half-life asymmetry ( 1.20×–5.30×) on checkpoints with
valid above-chance procedural baselines, demonstrated unconfounded 4b/3b backbone byte-parity gains on
Pythia-160M and SmolLM2-360M, revealed the necessity of MSE group clipping and gentler A WQ channel
scaling (α = 0.4 in 88.1% of projections) for 2–3-bit SwiGLU MLPs on Qwen2.5-3B and physical 8.00 GiB
RTX 4060 Qwen2.5-7B serving (3.84 GiB weights, 88.0% leak-free WikiText-2 calibrated retrieval cloze), and
scaled the pipeline to real 52-layer Muse-Glimmer-30B (29.78B parameters) via spectral proﬁling, Block-
Cholesky Hessian compensation ( −47.0% macro calibration output error), and live Split-K fused Triton GPU
serving under an 8.00 GiB VRAM wall ( 7.29 GiB PyTorch / 7.92 GiB nvidia-smi + 3.26 GiB host RAM,
9.0 tok/s). By pairing constructive systems engineering with a full forensic audit of every methodological
confound and negative result, we provide a transparent foundation for future semi-parametric low-bit edge
LLM research.
Broader Impact Statement
Enabling 7B–30B open-weight language models to execute within consumer 8.00 GiB GPU memory envelopes
lowers computational and ﬁnancial barriers for independent researchers, students, and privacy-sensitive local
applications where cloud APIs are cost-prohibitive or impermissible. Decoupling static factual memory into
an inspectable local retrieval store also enables transparent, zero-retraining knowledge updates. Conversely,
broader local oﬄine availability reduces centralized API-level safety oversight, creating dual-use misuse risks
if deployed without safeguards. Furthermore, we did not empirically evaluate safety alignment or
refusal behavior after sub-4-bit quantization ; prior work indicates aggressive low-bit compression can
degrade alignment guardrails. Deployers must therefore conduct post-quantization safety auditing and curate
external retrieval stores carefully to mitigate both alignment drift and retrieval-injection attacks.
